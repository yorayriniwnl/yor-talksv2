# Initial global regulatory intake — 7 October 2026

**Status: research inputs, not approved country policies.** Intended scope is an EU-based social app with global users and under-13, 13–17 and 18+ experiences. The operator's member state, entity, scale, providers and operating arrangements remain unknown. This intake covers selected regimes; it is not an exhaustive inventory of every national, state or provincial rule.

The three product bands cannot substitute for jurisdiction-specific ages. Relevant rules depend on the activity, legal basis, territory, business size and feature. Unreviewed territories and unaccepted features remain release gates under the draft [age/privacy design](superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-global-age-consent-design.md).

## EU operator and EU users

- GDPR Article 8 concerns consent-based information-society services offered directly to children. The default independent-consent threshold is 16; national law may lower it to 13. Below the applicable threshold, reasonable efforts to verify parental authorisation are required when that consent basis applies. This is not a universal minimum account age. Rights handling, lawful bases, minimisation, retention, processors/transfers and security require their own operating evidence. DPIA/DPO requirements depend on their statutory conditions. [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng).
- DSA hosting notice-and-action, reasons and user/authority contacts require applicability assessment. The micro/small-enterprise exemption in Article 19 covers most Section 3 duties, including Articles 20, 26 and 28, subject to its conditions and VLOP exception; do not automatically impose or waive duties based on the app's apparent size. [DSA](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj/eng). The Commission's [minor-protection guidelines](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors) are a reference, not automatic certification.
- AI transparency depends on the actual AI role and feature. Article 50 duties generally apply from 2 August 2026; a special transition for marking certain pre-existing systems does not defer every transparency duty. [Commission AI transparency guidance](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-ai-transparency-obligations).
- European Accessibility Act national measures apply from 28 June 2025 to covered services, including e-commerce and electronic communications. Microenterprises providing services are exempt when they meet the fewer-than-10-staff and €2 million financial criteria. Classify actual messaging and paid features and verify the member state's implementation. [Directive 2019/882, Articles 2–4 and 31](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj/eng).
- Consumer contracts require applicable pre-contract information, payment authorisation and withdrawal handling. National measures implementing the online withdrawal function apply from 19 June 2026 to online distance contracts subject to withdrawal rights. Keep the function accessible throughout the withdrawal period and provide durable confirmation. Withdrawal-period handling and ongoing subscription cancellation are distinct. [Directive 2023/2673](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32023L2673).

Needed facts: member state and national implementing laws, entity and group size, paid contract terms, AI/provider roles, actual processing and transfers, child access and moderation operations.

## United States

COPPA applies to covered commercial child-directed services and general-audience operators with actual knowledge of collection from under-13 users, including relevant foreign services. The amended rule's ordinary compliance deadline was 22 April 2026. Covered collection requires parental notice and verifiable parental consent before collection; non-integral third-party disclosure requires separate consent. Written security/retention controls and limits on indefinite retention need implementation. [Final amended rule](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm), [FTC compliance guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-six-step-compliance-plan-your-business).

State privacy, consumer, youth-safety and sector-specific duties remain a separate applicability review; this federal entry does not approve all US states. Needed facts: US targeting/access, actual under-13 collection, commercial model, processors/disclosures and state nexus.

## United Kingdom

In-scope services with UK links need child-access/risk assessment and appropriate protections against harmful content. Self-declared age and an 18+ terms clause do not establish highly effective age assurance. [Ofcom age assurance](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/age-assurance), [children's safety duties](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/statement-protecting-children-from-harms-online).

The ICO Children's Code concerns relevant services likely to be accessed by under-18s, including relevant overseas operators. The UK consent threshold of 13 is a separate question. [ICO code scope](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/introduction-to-the-childrens-code/). Needed facts: UK links, child access, processing, feature risk and assurance methods.

## Australia

Since 10 December 2025, covered social platforms must take reasonable steps to prevent accounts for under-16 users ordinarily resident in Australia. Parental permission cannot override this restriction. Apply the current criteria and exclusions, including the March 2026 recommender/logged-in criteria, to this app; being new or absent from a named-platform list does not settle coverage. [eSafety current criteria and FAQs](https://www.esafety.gov.au/about-us/industry-regulation/social-media-age-restrictions/faqs).

Needed facts: Australian availability/residence, significant social purpose, linking/posting functions, recommender/logged-in features and possible exclusions. A single 13–17 capability policy cannot implement this boundary.

## Canada, including Quebec

PIPEDA covers relevant commercial personal-information handling and international flows. Foreign operators require assessment of a real and substantial Canadian connection. Meaningful consent is required; federal law does not create a universal statutory age-13 cutoff. OPC guidance generally calls for parental consent below 13 and maturity-appropriate consent for older youth. [PIPEDA scope](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/), [OPC consent guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/p_principle/principles/p_consent/).

Quebec requires parental/tutor consent below 14; direct collection has a narrow clearly-for-the-child's-benefit exception. That collection provision applies from 22 September 2023. Covered enterprises also need relevant system/transfer privacy-impact assessments and highest-confidentiality defaults for technological services with privacy settings. [Quebec statute, sections 3.3, 4.1, 9.1, 14 and 17](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1).

Needed facts: commercial nexus, provinces served, advertising/profiling and processors/transfers. Provincial coverage must be assessed separately.

## Brazil

ECA Digital Law 15.211/2025 is effective from 17 March 2026 and covers services directed to or likely accessed by Brazilian minors regardless of operator location. Current Article 24 uses “de até 16” for accounts requiring linkage to a legal guardian: the literal wording includes age 16, so implementation must not silently assume an under-16 boundary. Article 22 prohibits profiling for commercial advertising to minors. Article 40 requires a Brazilian legal representative. Portuguese semiannual transparency reports become mandatory above one million registered Brazilian child/adolescent users. [Current consolidated law](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2025/lei/l15211.htm).

ANPD's implementation/inspection schedule does not defer the law's effective date. [ANPD implementation schedule](https://www.gov.br/anpd/pt-br/assuntos/eca-digital/). LGPD child processing requires best interests; ANPD permits applicable Article 7/11 legal bases, so parental consent is not the only basis for every operation. [ANPD binding interpretation](https://bibliotecadigital.mj.gov.br/bitstream/1/10215/2/Enunciado_ANPD_2023_1.html).

Needed facts: availability, current implementing guidance, guardian links, child-user count, advertising and representative. A product's 13–17 band does not resolve the guardian-link boundary.

## India

DPDP scope includes relevant processing outside India connected with offering goods/services to people in India; its child definition is under 18. Once the substantive provisions commence, Section 9 requires covered verifiable parental consent and restricts detrimental processing, tracking/behavioural monitoring and targeted child advertising, subject to prescribed exemptions. [DPDP Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf).

Core Data Fiduciary obligations are scheduled for 13 May 2027, rather than already fully active on this review date. The staged notification puts consent-manager provisions at 13 November 2026. [Commencement notification](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/c56ceae6c383460ca69577428d36828b.pdf), [government readiness timeline](https://negd.gov.in/events/dpdp-act-readiness-workshop-b2b-and-b2c-implementation/). Active intermediary and CERT-In requirements need assessment now. [Current IT amendment](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/02/f55fe52418b03f58b0669f6a8bc03b6d.pdf), [CERT-In directions](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf).

Needed facts: Indian offering/nexus, intermediary functions, infrastructure, users and applicable exemptions. The existing app's India-specific grievance labels do not establish either current Indian compliance or suitability for the EU operator.

## Before any territory is approved

Confirm actual applicability and local rules; select required assurance, notices and operating processes; implement the server policy; test direct and anonymous access, expiry/revocation and provider failure; and record the accountable approval and its evidence. A researched source URL alone cannot activate a country. Refresh this dated intake before release and whenever the service or relevant law changes.
